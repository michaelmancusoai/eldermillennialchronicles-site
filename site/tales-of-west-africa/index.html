Tales of West Africa

Forty West African Folk Tales, Told the Old Way

These are forty old tales of West Africa, told the old way: How the Stories Came to Anansi, Anansi and the Pot of Wisdom, Tortoise in the Drummer’s Sack, The Hyena Who Wrestled the Lizard, Ntyi and the Boa, and more.

Three books in one: The Spider’s Stories, The Tortoise’s Shell and Under the Silk-Cotton Tree, with one hundred and twenty ink-and-watercolour plates.

For Parents

Questions about Tales of West Africa

What is Tales of West Africa?

Tales of West Africa is a book in The Elder Millennial Chronicles: “Forty West African Folk Tales, Told the Old Way.” Every tale opens with its moral and keeps the consequences of the oldest versions. It is three books in one: The Spider’s Stories (13 tales), The Tortoise’s Shell (12 tales) and Under the Silk-Cotton Tree (15 tales), with 120 ink-and-watercolour plates, three for each tale.

Which tales are in Tales of West Africa?

The Spider’s Stories: How the Stories Came to Anansi, Anansi and the Pot of Wisdom, Thunder’s Pot and Thunder’s Stick, The Farm with No Road, The Blind Fisherman, Anansi’s Hat of Hot Beans, The Nodding Image, The Spider Who Borrowed Wings, Anansi Goes Looking for a Fool, The Thing in the Spider’s Trap, The Monkey Who Called Names, Anansi and the Rubber Man and The Stone That Ground by Itself. The Tortoise’s Shell: The Spotted Thing on the Anthill, The Worm Who Lent His Eyes, Tortoise in the Drummer’s Sack, The Tree Where Nobody Talked, The Hyena and the Three Feathers, The Bat Who Ate the Lamb’s Dinner, Turtle Rides the Leopard, The Tortoise Who Could Not Cry, The Hippo Nobody Could Name, Klo and the Palm Wine, The Hyena Who Wrestled the Lizard and Tortoise and the Cloud. Under the Silk-Cotton Tree: The Hunter and the Singing Tortoise, Ole and the Ants, Watch-Pot and Greedy, Remember the River Yom, Adzanumee, the Yam Daughter, The Parrot Who Said “She Lies”, The Three Gluttons, Ntyi and the Boa, The Man Who Told All His Secrets, Wait for Me, Big Sister, I Will Die God’s Death, The Leopard-Man, The Ape’s Kindness, The Hunter Who Became a Python and Abena Who Never Went Home.

Where do the tales in Tales of West Africa come from?

A few were written by people from the tradition itself. M. I. Ogumefu, a Yoruba writer, published her Yoruba Legends in 1929. And some of the tellers were named: Mohamma and Umoru Gombe, who told their Hausa tales to A. J. N. Tremearne; Malam Shaihu, a Hausa scholar, who wrote his down in Hausa for R. S. Rattray; Gave Ba, who told a Wolof tale at Dubréka in 1910; and Samako Niembélé, a Bambara interpreter, who told two tales at Bogandé in 1911.

What age is Tales of West Africa for?

Ages 6 to 12, read aloud with a grown-up. Each tale takes about three minutes to read. It opens with its moral, and the third plate stops one breath before the worst part, so the ending is understood rather than shown.

Is Tales of West Africa too scary for young children?

The tales keep their old endings: the greedy are punished and the careless pay for it. Nothing is shown past the turning point, and every tale says its lesson plainly first. If your child is very sensitive, read a tale yourself before reading it aloud. There is more on this in the For Parents section. Read the answers for parents.
