Tales of Central Africa

Forty Central African Folk Tales, Told the Old Way

For Parents

Questions about Tales of Central Africa

What is Tales of Central Africa?

Tales of Central Africa is a book in The Elder Millennial Chronicles: “Forty Central African Folk Tales, Told the Old Way.” Every tale opens with its moral and keeps the consequences of the oldest versions. It is three books in one: The Little Ones and the Leopard (13 tales), Why It Is So (13 tales) and Before the Elders (14 tales), with 120 ink-and-watercolour plates, three for each tale.

Which tales are in Tales of Central Africa?

The Little Ones and the Leopard: The Sparrow’s Tug-of-War, The Leopard Who Bowed to the Goat, The Leopard’s Wine-Booth, The Leopard Who Played Dead, Hare’s Long Basket, The Turtle Who Feared the Water, The Bird Who Copied the Owl, The Path That Was Caught, The Turtle’s Greedy Trap, The Ants Who Waited, The Elephant’s Son and the Crooked Guardian, The Leopard in the Goat’s Belly and The Leopard Who Wanted the Gazelle’s Wife. Why It Is So: Hog and His Cousin Boar, The Chicken Who Forgot the Fire, The Dog Who Came In from the Cold, The Water-Snake Who Went Fishing, The Plantain-Eater’s Nest, The Toad Who Invited the Ants, The Palm-Rat Who Was Deaf When Eating, Which Is the Fattest?, Dog and the Kingship, Cock and Fox, Fox and Mole, Partridge and Turtle and The King of the Hogs. Before the Elders: “It Is Mine!”, The Blacksmith and the Blackbirds, The Billy-Goat Lawsuit, Who Cried All Night, The Cassava Thieves, How Kengi Lost Her Child, The Imp’s Bargain, The Son Who Hid from His Father, Come Closer, Said the Crocodile, The Singing Dogs, The Cloud-Wife’s Basket, The Fish-Wife, The Spider Who Refused Every Suitor and The Little Sister and the Ma-kishi.

Where do the tales in Tales of Central Africa come from?

Héli Chatelain, a Swiss teacher and language scholar, took down the Mbundu tales of Angola in Kimbundu, mostly from his pupil Jeremiah of Malanje, and printed the words of the tellers beside his English in 1894. John H. Weeks, an English missionary, wrote down Kongo tales on the Lower Congo (1911) and Boloki tales far up the river (1913); the Boloki tellers wrote the morals themselves. Robert Hamill Nassau, an American doctor, heard the Mpongwe tales of Libreville from a man and a woman who were masters of the art (1912). R. E. Dennett, an English trader, recorded the Vili tales of the Loango coast (1898). Five tales come from the Kasai, recorded in German by Leo Frobenius in 1905–06 and printed in 1928; we have retold them from the German. And Emil Torday, a Hungarian traveller, heard the tale of the dog and the jackal in the Kuba country from the smith of Misumba, who told it at his work and gave the jackal a bark for a voice (1925).

What age is Tales of Central Africa for?

Ages 6 to 12, read aloud with a grown-up. Each tale takes about three minutes to read. It opens with its moral, and the third plate stops one breath before the worst part, so the ending is understood rather than shown.

Is Tales of Central Africa too scary for young children?

The tales keep their old endings: the greedy are punished and the careless pay for it. Nothing is shown past the turning point, and every tale says its lesson plainly first. If your child is very sensitive, read a tale yourself before reading it aloud. There is more on this in the For Parents section. Read the answers for parents.
