For Parents

Questions about Tales of the Jewish World

What is Tales of the Jewish World?

Tales of the Jewish World is a book in The Elder Millennial Chronicles: “Forty Jewish Folk Tales, Told the Old Way.” Every tale opens with its moral and keeps the consequences of the oldest versions. It is three books in one: The Clay Servant (14 tales), The Wise Men of Chelm (13 tales) and The Fox’s Counsel (13 tales), with 120 ink-and-watercolour plates, three for each tale.

Which tales are in Tales of the Jewish World?

The Clay Servant: The Golem of Prague, The Midwife and the Water King, The Locked Chest, The Envious Man and the Greedy Man, The Bird’s Three Lessons, The Devil Who Bought the Newest Thing, The Oath-Breaker and the Demon Queen, Bread upon the Waters, The Silver Apples and the Gold, The Dragon of Worms, Kunz and the King’s Three Questions, The Grateful Frog, The Money in the Tree and The Man Who Would Not Swear. The Wise Men of Chelm: The Golem Who Grew Too Tall, The Wise Men of Chelm, Hershele and the Hidden Supper, The Conjurer’s Pupil, The Mountain That Moved, The Teacher’s Goat, The Cow in the Loft, The Carp That Was Sentenced, The Logs That Wanted to Roll, The Lane That Was Too Narrow, The Night Chelm Burned, The Cat Who Came to Chelm and The Bear Who Wanted to Fly. The Fox’s Counsel: The Fox and the Leopard, The Fox Who Tied Up the Lion, The Washerwoman Who Outdid the Devil, The Judge and the Necklace, Half a Friend, The Thief’s Pomegranate Seed, The Lightest, the Sweetest, the Hardest, The Merchant Who Tried to Lose His Luck, Seven Good Years, Elijah the Builder, The Youngest Son’s Ten Friends, The Grateful Dead and The Language of the Ravens.

Where do the tales in Tales of the Jewish World come from?

We followed the old versions as closely as we could. Max Grunwald gathered the tales of the German Jewish towns in 1898, and Abraham Tendlau those of Frankfurt in 1856. Menahem Kipnis wrote down the tales of Chelm in Warsaw in 1930, and a rabbi in a small town on the Dnieper sent the bear’s fable to Grunwald in 1899. Leopold Weisel wrote down the Golem of Prague in 1847. Moses Gaster summarised hundreds of tales from old Hebrew manuscripts in 1924, and Israel Abrahams told Joseph ibn Zabara’s stories in English in 1912. Gertrude Landa and Gerald Friedlander retold many more for children a hundred years ago.

What age is Tales of the Jewish World for?

Ages 6 to 12, read aloud with a grown-up. Each tale takes about three minutes to read. It opens with its moral, and the third plate stops one breath before the worst part, so the ending is understood rather than shown.

Is Tales of the Jewish World too scary for young children?

The tales keep their old endings: the greedy are punished and the careless pay for it. Nothing is shown past the turning point, and every tale says its lesson plainly first. If your child is very sensitive, read a tale yourself before reading it aloud. There is more on this in the For Parents section. Read the answers for parents.

The Authors

Made by a father, checked by the two toughest critics in the house.

Michael A. Mancuso made these books for his own children, Gigi and Enzo, who helped decide which endings were scary enough and which jokes were funny enough. The result is a book you can read to a six-year-old tonight and still enjoy yourself the fortieth time through.
