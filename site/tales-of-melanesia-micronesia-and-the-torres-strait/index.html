Forty Melanesian and Micronesian Folk Tales, Told the Old Way

For Parents

Questions about Tales of Melanesia, Micronesia and the Torres Strait

What is Tales of Melanesia, Micronesia and the Torres Strait?

Tales of Melanesia, Micronesia and the Torres Strait is a book in The Elder Millennial Chronicles: “Forty Melanesian and Micronesian Folk Tales, Told the Old Way.” Every tale opens with its moral and keeps the consequences of the oldest versions. It is three books in one: The Tide on the Reef (13 tales), The Sea Behind the Fence (14 tales) and The Shared Canoe (13 tales), with 120 ink-and-watercolour plates, three for each tale.

Which tales are in Tales of Melanesia, Micronesia and the Torres Strait?

The Tide on the Reef: The Six Blind Brothers, Bébi and the Two Wild Pigeons, The Two Sisters and the Crab, The Cheat Who Grabbed Both Iguanas, The Banana Trees That Cried, The Girl with Sores and the Kind Little Sister, The Man Without Firewood, The Turtle Who Wore the Bowl, The Lizard Who Held His Breath, The House of Mud, The Brush Turkeys, Kwahohofi and the Great Wave and The Greedy Cave Dwellers. The Sea Behind the Fence: Kapikoa’s Betel Husks, The Flute in the Corkwood Tree, The Knot on the Waoi, The Ant in the House of Leaves, The Man Who Picked Fruit Among the Dead, The Wallaby on the Reef, The Cassowary Who Ate the Whole Tree, The Fisherman and the Voice in the Dark, The Heron Who Paid His Debt, The Rat and the Rail, The Pukeke and the Fowl, Tepete and the Ogress, Pwiopwio and the Fire-sticks and The Sea Behind the Fence. The Shared Canoe: The Sapless Bamboo, The Song for the Deaf Grandmother, One-Tooth Under the Rock, The Hawk Who Ate Everything, The Mosquitoes of Oneata, The Four Chiefs Who Followed the Sun, The Whale Who Wanted a Bow, The Mouse Who Would Not Share, Möqi and the Breadfruit Thief, The Mosquito Stone, Asgordo and the Giant Donao, The Ogress Who Said She Was Granny and The Girl Who Chose Perfume.

Where do the tales in Tales of Melanesia, Micronesia and the Torres Strait come from?

The islands in this book stretch across a quarter of the Pacific, from the Torres Strait between New Guinea and Cape York to the atolls of the Marshall Islands. Hundreds of peoples live there, speaking more languages than anywhere else on earth. Every tale in this book is labelled with the people and the place where it was written down, because that is the honest thing to say about it, and because the tellers were proud of their own islands.

What age is Tales of Melanesia, Micronesia and the Torres Strait for?

Ages 6 to 12, read aloud with a grown-up. Each tale takes about three minutes to read. It opens with its moral, and the third plate stops one breath before the worst part, so the ending is understood rather than shown.

Is Tales of Melanesia, Micronesia and the Torres Strait too scary for young children?

The tales keep their old endings: the greedy are punished and the careless pay for it. Nothing is shown past the turning point, and every tale says its lesson plainly first. If your child is very sensitive, read a tale yourself before reading it aloud. There is more on this in the For Parents section. Read the answers for parents.
