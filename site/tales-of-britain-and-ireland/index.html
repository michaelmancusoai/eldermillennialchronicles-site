For Parents

Questions about Tales of Britain and Ireland

What is Tales of Britain and Ireland?

Tales of Britain and Ireland is a book in The Elder Millennial Chronicles: “Forty British and Irish Folk Tales, Told the Old Way.” Every tale opens with its moral and keeps the consequences of the oldest versions. It is three books in one: The Fair Folk (14 tales), Bogles and Worms (14 tales) and The Long Ago (12 tales), with 120 ink-and-watercolour plates, three for each tale.

Which tales are in Tales of Britain and Ireland?

The Fair Folk: The Lake Maiden of Myddfai, Fairy Ointment, The Field of Boliauns, The Small People’s Cow, Kate Crackernuts, The Miser on the Gump, The Pwca’s Supper, The Brownie o’ Ferne-Den, The Knockers’ Tenth, My Own Self, The White Cow of Llyn Barfog, The Fairies of Merlin’s Crag, The Seal Hunter’s Oath and Tamlane. Bogles and Worms: Mr Miacca, The Horned Women, The Kelpie’s Bridle, The Hedley Kow, The Moddey Dhoo, The Red Etin, Selena Moor, Vengeance Cometh, The Buggane of St Trinian’s, Childe Rowland, Tehi Tegi, The Black Bull of Norroway, Canonbie Dick and The Golden Arm. The Long Ago: How the Herring Became King, The Salmon of Knowledge, The Cormorant and the Bat, The Hound of Culann, King O’Toole and His Goose, Fionn’s Wife and the Giant, Whittington and His Cat, The Well of Fior Usga, The Laidly Worm, The Drowned Hundred, Cap o’ Rushes and The Lambton Worm.

Where do the tales in Tales of Britain and Ireland come from?

Six peoples share these islands: the English, the Irish, the Manx, the Scots, the Cornish and the Welsh. They have told stories to one another for as long as anyone knows, and they borrowed freely. You will find a fairy cow in Wales and another in Cornwall, and a brave girl rescuing someone she loves on both sides of the Border. Every tale in this book is labelled with the people and the place where it was written down, because that is the honest thing to say about it, and because the tellers were proud of their own hills.

What age is Tales of Britain and Ireland for?

Ages 6 to 12, read aloud with a grown-up. Each tale takes about three minutes to read. It opens with its moral, and the third plate stops one breath before the worst part, so the ending is understood rather than shown.

Is Tales of Britain and Ireland too scary for young children?

The tales keep their old endings: the greedy are punished and the careless pay for it. Nothing is shown past the turning point, and every tale says its lesson plainly first. If your child is very sensitive, read a tale yourself before reading it aloud. There is more on this in the For Parents section. Read the answers for parents.
