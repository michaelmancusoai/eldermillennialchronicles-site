For Parents

Questions about Tales of East Africa and the Horn

What is Tales of East Africa and the Horn?

Tales of East Africa and the Horn is a book in The Elder Millennial Chronicles: “Forty East African Folk Tales, Told the Old Way.” Every tale opens with its moral and keeps the consequences of the oldest versions. It is three books in one: The Rubbish Heap (13 tales), The Little Red Bird (17 tales) and The Axe of Clay (10 tales), with 120 ink-and-watercolour plates, three for each tale.

Which tales are in Tales of East Africa and the Horn?

The Rubbish Heap: The Gazelle Who Made a Sultan, The Donkey Who Came Back, The Father’s Blessing, Ha! and Hi!, Goso the Teacher, The Hare Who Was Thinking, The Copper Needle, The Little Eel Who Played Dead, The Crocodile and the Pig, Fine Clothes and Rough Cloth, The Cat Who Broke the Oath, The Wild Ox at the Spring and The Two Thieves and the Old Woman. The Little Red Bird: The Hare Who Pretended to Dig, The Hyenas’ New Prophet, The Elephant Who Let the First Calf Go, Don’t Look Back, Who Is in the Hare’s House?, The Coward and the Monkey, What Made Mawenzi Jagged, The Banana-Leaf Bundles, After the Harvest, Carve Your Spoons, The Bird with the Broken Leg, The Hyenas Who Flew for Fat, The Hyena Who Was Trusted, Kasanke, the Little Red Bird, The Foolish Hare, The Pot, the Drum and the Monkey, Walukaga the Blacksmith and Why the Bat Hides from the Sun. The Axe of Clay: The Axe of Clay, The Leopard Who Blamed the Goats, The Two Travellers, The Donkey Who Had to Bray, The Boar Who Took Advice, The Hyena Who Lost His Herd, When the Leopard Grows Old, The Slap on the Hyena’s Cheek, The Pot That Ate the Mutton and The Vizier’s Letter.

Where do the tales in Tales of East Africa and the Horn come from?

Every tale in this book was told aloud for a long, long time before anybody wrote it down. Most were written down between about 1870 and 1925, by people who sat with the tellers and listened.

What age is Tales of East Africa and the Horn for?

Ages 6 to 12, read aloud with a grown-up. Each tale takes about three minutes to read. It opens with its moral, and the third plate stops one breath before the worst part, so the ending is understood rather than shown.

Is Tales of East Africa and the Horn too scary for young children?

The tales keep their old endings: the greedy are punished and the careless pay for it. Nothing is shown past the turning point, and every tale says its lesson plainly first. If your child is very sensitive, read a tale yourself before reading it aloud. There is more on this in the For Parents section. Read the answers for parents.

The Authors

Made by a father, checked by the two toughest critics in the house.

Michael A. Mancuso made these books for his own children, Gigi and Enzo, who helped decide which endings were scary enough and which jokes were funny enough. The result is a book you can read to a six-year-old tonight and still enjoy yourself the fortieth time through.
