For Parents

Questions about Tales of Iberia

What is Tales of Iberia?

Tales of Iberia is a book in The Elder Millennial Chronicles: “Forty Iberian Folk Tales, Told the Old Way.” Every tale opens with its moral and keeps the consequences of the oldest versions. It is three books in one: The Granny’s Gourd (13 tales), The Little Box (13 tales) and The Lamiña’s Comb (14 tales), with 120 ink-and-watercolour plates, three for each tale.

Which tales are in Tales of Iberia?

The Granny’s Gourd: The Mouse with the Blue Ribbon, The Fox’s Harvest, The Rooster Who Took Advice, The Ant and the Goat in the House, The Rabbit and the Cat’s Tail, Why Dogs Sniff, The Wolves on Each Other’s Backs, The Little Ant and Pérez the Mouse, Patufet, Grain-of-Maize, The Promise at the Door, The Singing Sack and The Granny in the Gourd. The Little Box: The Little Box, The Horseshoe and the Cherries, Good Luck Asleep, The Price of the Eggs, The Laborer’s Half, Juan Cigarrón, The Man Who Hated Warnings, The Clever Lad and the Moneylender, The Flour and the Wind, The Magic Mouthful, The Ox Called Rabil, Troubles Young or Old and Same Measure, Same Weight. The Lamiña’s Comb: The Lamiña’s Golden Comb, The Bridge at Licq, The Lamiña by the Fire, The Midwife’s Golden Pear, The Miller’s Share, The Three Truths, The Lady Who Fed the Pigs, The Goblin Hen, The Goblin Who Kneaded Bread, The Devil’s Age, The Brother in the Witches’ Tree, The Witches’ Word, The Lamiña at the Spring and Juan Holgado and Death.

Where do the tales in Tales of Iberia come from?

Fernán Caballero was the pen name of Cecilia Böhl de Faber, who spent her life in Andalusia and wrote down the nursery tales she heard in its villages, sometimes from an old village storyteller she called Uncle Romance. Adolfo Coelho gathered Portuguese tales and wrote beside each one the place it came from: Coimbra, Bragança, the Minho, the mouth of the Douro. Francesc Maspons i Labrós printed the first great book of Catalan tales. Jean-François Cerquand asked the village schoolteachers of the Basque mountains to write down what the old people told them, and printed the answers in four parts, with the names of the houses and the villages. Wentworth Webster and Julien Vinson wrote Basque tales down from named storytellers, among them Estefanella Hirigaray of Ahetze and Franchun Beltzarri. Consiglieri Pedroso’s Portuguese tales were put into English in 1882. Elsie Spicer Eells, an American, heard the tales of the Azores and retold them in 1922. Eugenio de Olavarría wrote down the tales Madrid told about the Lord and Saint Peter on the road. In the green hills of Asturias, a storyteller called la Rosa told her tales at Proaza to L. Giner Arivau, and Aurelio de Llano went from village to village asking the farmers what they knew.

What age is Tales of Iberia for?

Ages 6 to 12, read aloud with a grown-up. Each tale takes about three minutes to read. It opens with its moral, and the third plate stops one breath before the worst part, so the ending is understood rather than shown.

Is Tales of Iberia too scary for young children?

The tales keep their old endings: the greedy are punished and the careless pay for it. Nothing is shown past the turning point, and every tale says its lesson plainly first. If your child is very sensitive, read a tale yourself before reading it aloud. There is more on this in the For Parents section. Read the answers for parents.
