For Parents

Questions about Tales of the Caribbean & African America

What is Tales of the Caribbean & African America?

Tales of the Caribbean & African America is a book in The Elder Millennial Chronicles: “Forty Caribbean and African American Folk Tales, Told the Old Way.” Every tale opens with its moral and keeps the consequences of the oldest versions. It is three books in one: Anansi’s Rope (17 tales), The Briar Patch (12 tales) and Haunts and Fools (11 tales), with 120 ink-and-watercolour plates, three for each tale.

Which tales are in Tales of the Caribbean & African America?

Anansi’s Rope: Anansi and the Queen Named Five, The Man-Crow, Ticky-Picky Boom-Boom, Rat’s Wedding, The Cats’ Ball, Anansi and Candlefly, Snake the Postman, Crab’s Corn-Piece, Leap, Timber, Leap, Anansi in Crab Country, Gaulin’s Wedding, Anansi and Tiger at the Blue Hole, Anansi and the Roasted Plantains, Anansi and the Cows, Tumble-bug’s Butter, The Horn-Tribe Party and Fling-a-Mile. The Briar Patch: Brer Wolf and the Two Dinners, Brer Squirrel’s Grace, Brer Owl and Brer Rooster, Brer Rabbit’s Rock Soup, Little Ox and Bouki, Brer Monkey Goes Looking for Trouble, Fowl-Hawk’s Boast, Inside the Cow, Brer Partridge’s Share, Brer Rabbit and the Ground-Mole, The Broken Bargain and Brother Lion’s Pap. Haunts and Fools: Dry River, Jogi Stays Awake, Watchman, Lion and Tiger, The Lard Girl, The Parrot Who Saw, Juan Bobo Sends the Pig to Mass, Juan Bobo Saves Himself Some Work, Mr. Rainy-Day, The Girl Who Chose a Stranger, Two Rocks and a Monkey’s Head and The Fiddler and the Dancing Beasts.

Where do the tales in Tales of the Caribbean & African America come from?

Walter Jekyll, an Englishman living in the Port Royal Mountains of Jamaica, printed his neighbours’ stories and songs in 1907. Pamela Colman Smith, an artist who grew up partly in Kingston, retold Jamaican tales in 1899. Martha Warren Beckwith went from yard to yard in Jamaica and named every teller she heard. Elsie Clews Parsons did the same on Andros Island in the Bahamas, on South Carolina’s Sea Islands, and later in Trinidad and Haiti, and set down each teller’s name, age and settlement. Charles C. Jones wrote down the Gullah tales of the Georgia coast in 1888. J. Alden Mason and Aurelio Espinosa recorded Puerto Rico’s tales in Spanish, and Manuel Andrade those of the Dominican Republic.

What age is Tales of the Caribbean & African America for?

Ages 6 to 12, read aloud with a grown-up. Each tale takes about three minutes to read. It opens with its moral, and the third plate stops one breath before the worst part, so the ending is understood rather than shown.

Is Tales of the Caribbean & African America too scary for young children?

The tales keep their old endings: the greedy are punished and the careless pay for it. Nothing is shown past the turning point, and every tale says its lesson plainly first. If your child is very sensitive, read a tale yourself before reading it aloud. There is more on this in the For Parents section. Read the answers for parents.
