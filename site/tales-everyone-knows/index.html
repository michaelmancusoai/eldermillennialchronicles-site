For Parents

Questions about The Tales Everyone Knows

What is The Tales Everyone Knows?

The Tales Everyone Knows is a book in The Elder Millennial Chronicles: “Forty Fables, Fairy Tales and Cautionary Tales, Told the Old Way.” Every tale opens with its moral and keeps the consequences of the oldest versions. It is three books in one: Fables (13 tales), Fairy Tales (17 tales) and Cautionary Tales (10 tales), with 120 ink-and-watercolour plates, three for each tale.

Which tales are in The Tales Everyone Knows?

Fables: The Boy Who Cried Wolf, The Tortoise and the Hare, The Ant and the Grasshopper, The Fox and the Crow, The Three Little Pigs, The Little Red Hen, The Goose That Laid the Golden Eggs, The Milkmaid and Her Pail, Henny Penny, The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse, The Wolf in Sheep’s Clothing, The Frogs Who Wanted a King and The Scorpion and the Frog. Fairy Tales: Little Red Riding Hood, Hansel and Gretel, The Frog Prince, Jack and the Beanstalk, The Ugly Duckling, The Three Billy Goats Gruff, Mother Holle, The Wolf and the Seven Young Goats, Rumpelstiltskin, The Fisherman and His Wife, Goldilocks and the Three Bears, Diamonds and Toads, Cinderella (The Original), The Goose Girl, Snow White (The Original), The Pied Piper of Hamelin and The Bremen Town Musicians. Cautionary Tales: The Gingerbread Man, Hans in Luck, Humpty Dumpty, The Emperor’s New Clothes, King Midas, Johnny Head-in-Air, Harriet and the Matches, Icarus, The Swineherd and Pandora’s Box.

Where do the tales in The Tales Everyone Knows come from?

The fables are mostly those credited to Aesop. The milkmaid’s version is Jean de La Fontaine’s, of 1678. Little Red Riding Hood and Diamonds and Toads come from Charles Perrault’s collection of 1697, in which there is no woodsman at all. Most of the other fairy tales follow the Brothers Grimm. Jack is English; the billy goats are Norwegian; Goldilocks began, in Robert Southey’s story of 1837, as a nasty old woman. Midas, Icarus and Pandora are Greek myths; the Emperor, the Ugly Duckling and the Swineherd are Hans Christian Andersen’s; Johnny Head-in-Air and Harriet come from Heinrich Hoffmann’s Struwwelpeter of 1845.

What age is The Tales Everyone Knows for?

Ages 6 to 12, read aloud with a grown-up. Each tale takes about three minutes to read. It opens with its moral, and the third plate stops one breath before the worst part, so the ending is understood rather than shown.

Is The Tales Everyone Knows too scary for young children?

The tales keep their old endings: the greedy are punished and the careless pay for it. Nothing is shown past the turning point, and every tale says its lesson plainly first. If your child is very sensitive, read a tale yourself before reading it aloud. There is more on this in the For Parents section. Read the answers for parents.
